# CoLab MRR Pricing Model
Built July 27, 2026. Replaces earlier build-fee-first structure.

---

## FIRST, THE HONEST ANSWER ON "QUADRUPLE"

The earlier comparison was misleading. $2,997 versus $10,561 is not the same product at two prices.

Break it apart:

Own it: $2,997 buys a build. That is it. After handoff you are on your own.

On plan: $997 down plus $797/mo for 12 months = $10,561. Of that, roughly $2,000 is the deferred build cost. The remaining $8,561 buys twelve months of someone building, fixing, and improving the system.

So the real question is not "why is it 4x." It is "is a year of ongoing technical work worth about $700 a month."

That question has a clean answer, but only if the monthly deliverable is defined. Undefined, $797 is indefensible. Defined, it is cheap. Everything below exists to define it.

---

## WHAT THE MONTHLY ACTUALLY BUYS

The unit is a build request. One specific thing they want changed, added, or automated. Countable, deliverable, and it is the thing they cannot get anywhere else.

Every retainer includes:
- Hosting, database, uptime
- Bug fixes, unlimited
- Email and text support
- Security and dependency updates

Tiers differ by build requests per month and strategy time.

A build request looks like: add a new pipeline stage, build a follow up sequence for expired listings, connect Zillow leads to the CRM, add a report showing which lead source actually closes, automate the TC checklist handoff, add a new user role.

Roughly two hours of your time each, using Claude Code. That is the whole reason this model works. A dev shop would bill $500 to $1,500 for the same request.

---

## THE COMPARISON THAT JUSTIFIES IT

Do not compare Edge to software. You lose that comparison. Compare it to labor.

Part time VA: $800 to $1,500/mo. Cannot build anything.
ISA: $2,000 to $4,000/mo. Cannot build anything.
Freelance developer: $75 to $150/hr. Four hours a month is $300 to $600 and they do not know real estate.
Agency retainer: $2,800 to $7,000/mo at the SMB median.

Edge at $697 is a technical person on retainer who already knows the business, for less than a part time assistant.

That is the pitch. Not "cheaper than Follow Up Boss." It is not software.

## SEGMENTED STRUCTURE

Three segments. Two payment paths inside each. Six combinations total, but the client only ever sees two, because their segment is obvious from the discovery call.

---

### SOLO
One person. One login. No team.

Build: Spark, one tool, or a simple single user CRM

Own it: $997 build. Care at $197/mo optional after.
On plan: $297 down, then $397/mo for 12 months.

What $397 includes:
- One build request per month
- Everything in Care
- Quarterly 30 minute call

Year one on plan: $5,061
Year one own it: $997 plus optional $2,364 Care = $3,361

---

### TEAM
Two to nine people. Multiple roles. Coordinator, admin, agents.

Build: System, full custom CRM with role based access

Own it: $2,997 build. Care at $297/mo optional after.
On plan: $997 down, then $697/mo for 12 months.

What $697 includes:
- Two build requests per month
- Everything in Care
- Monthly 30 minute strategy call
- Team onboarding for new hires

Year one on plan: $9,361
Year one own it: $2,997 plus optional $3,564 Care = $6,561

---

### BROKERAGE
Ten plus. Office or multi office.

Build: Custom scope

Own it: $8,000 to $15,000 depending on scope and profiles.
On plan: $2,997 down, then $1,497/mo for 12 months.

What $1,497 includes:
- Four build requests per month
- Everything in Care
- Monthly strategy call plus quarterly review with leadership
- Agent onboarding and training sessions
- Office level reporting

Year one on plan: $20,961

## CARE, THE ANTI CHURN TIER

$197 solo, $297 team, $497 brokerage.

Hosting, database, uptime, bug fixes, support. No build requests.

This exists for one reason. When someone wants to cancel because money got tight, you do not lose them, you downgrade them. $297 a month forever beats $697 for four months and then zero.

Never let a cancellation happen without offering Care first. This single mechanic protects more MRR than any price change.

---

## HOW YOU ENSURE IT

The risk with a defined deliverable is that you owe something every month whether or not they ask.

Rules that protect you:

1. Build requests do not roll over. Use it or lose it, stated up front. Otherwise you accumulate debt and month nine is a nightmare.

2. A build request is scoped by you, not them. If they ask for something that is really three requests, you say that, and it takes three months or they add on. Say this on day one so it is not a fight later.

3. Anything outside scope is quoted separately. New integrations with per use API costs, data migrations, full redesigns.

4. Twelve month term on plan pricing, month to month on Care. The term is what makes the discounted build possible, and you should say exactly that: the build is discounted because the commitment covers it.

5. Cap at five on plan clients. Team tier at two requests a month is roughly four hours. Five clients is twenty hours a month. When five are full, raise the price instead of taking a sixth.

## WHAT THIS DOES TO YOUR MRR

Five team clients on plan: $3,485/mo, $41,820/yr recurring.
Same five on the old model, $497 Edge with maybe half attaching: $1,243/mo.

Nearly triple, and it comes from a lower barrier to entry, not a higher price. That is the actual mechanism. $997 down is an easier yes than $2,997, so more people say yes, and every yes is now recurring instead of one time.

---

## THE HONEST RISK

You are trading cash today for cash later. On plan, a team client pays $997 up front for a build that takes real hours. If they churn at month three you have collected $2,388 for a $3,000 build.

Three protections:

Twelve month term, stated in writing. Not a contract you would sue over, but it sets the expectation and most people honor what they agreed to.

Do not offer on plan to someone who feels shaky. Discovery call is where you decide. If they are hesitant about everything, sell them own it and take the cash.

Do not run more than five on plan clients until you have hard data on your real monthly hours. Track Michael, Stu, and Mike for 30 days first.

## WHAT STILL NEEDS TO BE TRUE

You have never delivered a defined monthly build request to anyone. That is the untested part of this model, not the pricing.

Before rolling this out broadly, run it with one client for 60 days and find out what two build requests a month actually costs you in time. Heather is a reasonable first test.

If it turns out two requests is eight hours instead of four, the price goes up or the requests go down. Better to learn that on client one than client five.
